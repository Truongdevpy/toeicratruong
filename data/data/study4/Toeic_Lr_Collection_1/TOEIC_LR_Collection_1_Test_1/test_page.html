By Rosanne Villalobos

THISTLETON (6 February)—Larchmere Print and Packaging will move its operations to a larger site north of town, the firm confirmed this week. Its Wesley Street premises can no longer hold the presses needed to meet rising orders for folded cartons. The new building at Hollowbrook Industrial Park offers 4,200 square meters, nearly twice the present production area.

"We have been turning work away for want of room for a fourth press," said Ingrid Solberg, the operations manager. "How orders are taken and handled will not alter, and no counter post will be lost."

The handover will proceed in three stages. In the first, beginning on 2 March, contractors will fit out the new building. In the second, from 20 April, company engineers will move the presses across, and although production will pause briefly, no order deadline will be affected. In the third, beginning on 1 June, the order collection point will transfer to Hollowbrook.

Ordering by telephone or through the online portal remains unchanged, as do current prices and counter opening hours. The one difference for customers is that from the third stage, finished work must be collected at Hollowbrook rather than on Wesley Street. The firm also plans to hire 12 production staff. Applications close on 21 February and should be sent to its personnel office.

Castleford Bike Share

Two Wheels, One City

Castleford Bike Share lets residents borrow a bicycle from one docking station and leave it at any other. Bikes are available around the clock at more than 40 docking stations spread across the city, and stations are checked and restocked each morning.

How It Works

Step 1: Create an account online and choose a plan.
Step 2: At any station, tap your member card at the kiosk. The kiosk is run entirely by the app, so no staff are on site.
Step 3: Take the unlocked bike and ride wherever you like. The first 45 minutes of every trip are free, and longer trips add a small charge.
Step 4: Return the bike to any open dock and press the lock button to end your trip.

Membership Options

Riders must be at least 16 years old. Registration is completed entirely online, and each member unlocks bikes using either the member card or the mobile app.

Report a Problem

If a bike is damaged or a dock will not release, open the app and tap the Report a Fault button to send us a short online form. Our team reviews these reports throughout the day. To recover a lost item, telephone the operations center during office hours.

Thornhedge Nursery Welcomes Visitors to Its Second Store

ARDENFORD (14 March) - Thornhedge Nursery, a family-run garden business known across the region for its wide selection of shrubs and flowering plants, has opened a second store on Wexley Lane in Ardenford.

The business began eleven years ago as a single stall selling potted herbs at a weekend market. Yvonne Castellan, who started Thornhedge and continues to oversee its day-to-day operations, said the company's steady growth had come from modest beginnings. "We had only a small table and a few dozen plants when we opened," she recalled. "Every step since then has been guided by what our customers ask us for, and this new store is the clearest sign yet of how far that has taken us."

The Ardenford store occupies a converted warehouse and features a bright indoor display hall, a covered courtyard for larger trees, and a separate room set aside for teaching. Wide aisles allow visitors to move easily between the seasonal arrangements, and a central advice counter is staffed throughout the day so that shoppers can ask about plant care before they buy.

The new location also introduces a program of hands-on planting workshops, something the first store was never able to accommodate. Sessions are held twice each week, and each one is limited to sixteen participants so that staff can give individual attention. Topics range from container gardening to the care of indoor foliage.

The store is open every day except Tuesday, from nine in the morning until six in the evening. General entry carries no charge, and throughout the opening month the nursery is offering each visitor a complimentary young seedling to take home.

"A garden center should be a place where people feel welcome to learn, not only to buy," Ms. Castellan said. "I hope this store gives our neighbours somewhere to spend an afternoon and to leave with something they have grown themselves."
