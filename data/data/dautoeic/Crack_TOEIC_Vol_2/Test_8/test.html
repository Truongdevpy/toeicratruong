Organising a Company Retreat That Works

By Imogen Halvard

A badly planned retreat can do lasting damage inside a company. It usually results from a one-size-fits-all approach. In other words, the organiser wrongly assumes that a format that worked once will work every time. Instead, you must shape your plan around the particular occasion. Here are some pointers:

Aims: You must first be clear about what the retreat is meant to achieve. It might be to reassure staff, to explain a decision, to gather feedback, or even to mark an achievement. The aim will set the tone of the days as well as the activities.

Participants: A company-wide away day and an annual awards weekend will naturally involve staff at every level. However, a retreat that follows a departmental restructure will go far more smoothly if only line managers attend, and an induction retreat for new starters will feel awkward if directors join.

Timing: A useful rule of thumb is that no company retreat should run longer than two days. But bear in mind that the right length depends on what the retreat is for. Half a day can feel endless in some cases, while three days will pass quickly in others.
